Material flow curves

First-order estimates. Body motion is Stokes flow of a viscous drop of equal volume in an unbounded fluid, layer by layer, using your material's viscosity where one is chosen and the required viscosity otherwise; walls, the free surface, neighbouring layers and shape change are ignored. In the flow-curve panel the dashed line is the model strain rate and each black tick is that layer's required viscosity: a rising curve thickens, a falling one thins.

Flow-law library

On the published site this table is read from data/flowlaws.json, which the CMS edits. A in MPa⁻ⁿ µmᵖ s⁻¹ (with the water term in its listed unit), E in kJ/mol, V in cm³/mol. "Parameters confirmed" means A, n and Q (and p, r, V where the law has them) were matched to the source's table or abstract, or to a published compilation that quotes it. "A to verify" and "verify against source" mark values entered from memory of standard compilations: check them against the original table before they go into a paper. Any law can be overridden per layer.

Lab materials library

Indicative values to start from: measure your own batches. Viscosity follows η = η₀ (ε̇/ε̇₀)^(1/n − 1) exp[Eₐ/R (1/T − 1/T₀)], so n above 1 is shear-thinning and n below 1 shear-thickening. Picking a material in a layer copies these values there, where they can be changed.

Validation

Every test

Runs each time the page opens. "Published value" reproduces numbers printed in the source. "Lab window" asks whether a law predicts a believable laboratory stress at laboratory temperature and strain rate: it catches unit errors, not a factor-of-few error in A. The remaining groups test the code against closed-form results.

Method

Flow law. ε̇ = A σⁿ d⁻ᵖ Wʳ exp(−(E + PV)/RT) for each mechanism. Dislocation and diffusion creep act in parallel: at each depth the stress is solved so that ε̇_disl + ε̇_diff equals the imposed strain rate, and η = σ_II / 2ε̇_II.

Lab-data conversion. Axial-compression laws are converted to second invariants with ε̇_II = (3^((n+1)/2) / 2) A σ_IIⁿ; simple-shear laws with ε̇_II = 2^(n−1) A σ_IIⁿ.

Geotherm. T(z) = min(T_s + G z, T_p + G_a z). Pressure is lithostatic from the layer densities.

Brittle strength. Δσ = C₀ + β ρ g z (1 − λ), compared with the viscous differential stress taken as 2σ_II. Where brittle is weaker, the layer is counted as brittle and left out of the viscosity average.

Scaling. With starred ratios model/nature: σ* = ρ* g* L*, η* = σ* t*, v* = L*/t*, ε̇* = 1/t*. Required model viscosity is η_nature · η*; a model cohesion scales with σ*.

Limits. A Newtonian analogue matches a power-law layer only at the reference strain rate. Low-temperature (Peierls) plasticity, grain-boundary sliding, melt and grain-size evolution are not included; the viscosity cut-off stands in for the cold, strong part of each layer.
